9 · the scrollbar

The browser's own, and the last borrowed thing on the page. The track is a ruled line generated with the same pen as everything else and handed to CSS as a data URI; the thumb is a pencil stub seen end-on, graphite with a rust cap. Scroll the box, and the page's own bar on the right is the same.

A collar goes on an animal someone could catch, in a place they could reach, in a year that was funded. That is why no track source in this project can carry a trend: effort is not a measured denominator, and the places a collar reaches are not a sample of anywhere.

The radar is different. A weather radar is fixed infrastructure running one protocol every night whether or not anybody is watching, which is the only reason thirty-one years of it can be compared with itself.

And a trawl survey is different again: the effort is the design. The same gear, the same stations, the same season, for sixty years — which is what made the marine null worth trusting when it came back saying nothing had happened.

10 · the type

Seven pairings, all self-hosted and all OFL, at the same generous spacing so what is being compared is the letterforms rather than one having been given more room. 11 · type as a setting

The default is hand-drawn everywhere, small parts included. The other two are not preferences: someone with dyslexia or low vision needs a way out that is one click, not a browser extension. Each face carries its own scale and leading, because they do not share an x-height — so switching changes the letterforms and nothing else.
