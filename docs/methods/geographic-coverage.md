## The structural problem, which is worse than "it is American"

**The two sources with global coverage are exactly the two that cannot support change detection.**
MegaMove is one pooled 1985–2018 interval and OBIS speciesgrids is one row per taxon-cell with a
year range — both established in `phase1b-marine.md`. Every source with a usable time axis is
northern-temperate, and every result in Phase 1 therefore is:

- The aerial phenology result is one country's radar network.
- The marine distribution result spans the US, Canada and Europe — better than one country, still
  0% southern hemisphere and 0% tropics.
- Global extent and change are, at present, disjoint properties of this lake.

FISHGLOB reaching 82°N and −180 → 180 in longitude flatters it: that is Norwegian Arctic shelf plus
Pacific and Atlantic North America, not global reach.

## Where the bias came from

Two different causes, worth separating because only one is fixable by choosing better sources.

**Inherited.** Weather-radar biological products exist where radar networks were digitised and
opened, which is North America and Europe. Standardised multi-decade trawl series exist where
fisheries agencies were funded to run them. Neither is a choice this project made.

**Self-inflicted.** eBird Status and Trends covers the world, and the ingest restricts it to the
contiguous US — a deliberate scoping decision for the radar comparison, documented in
`ingest/ebird_st.py`, and the reason that row reads 0.0% / 0.0%. Widening it is a constant, not a
new source. The 50-species cap in the eBird Terms binds either way, so a global composite would
mean trading North American species for others rather than adding.

## Candidates, researched

**Aerial, Europe — reassessed, and it cannot do the job it was picked for.** The
[ENRAM / aloftdata repository](https://enram.github.io/data-repository/) publishes vertical
profiles of bird density and flight speed from ~190 weather radars, generated by `vol2bird`, with a
2025 *Scientific Data* descriptor ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04641-5)).

This note originally said it "would turn the autumn-advance result from one network's finding into a
two-network comparison". **That was my claim and it is wrong.** Measured from the bucket's own
`coverage.csv`, which indexes every radar-day, on 2026-07-30:

| autumns with a usable record | radars |
| --- | --- |
| ≥ 5 | 87 |
| ≥ 8 | 23 |
| ≥ 10 | 3 |
| ≥ 15 | **1** |

The network only becomes broad recently — 1 to 5 radars per autumn until 2016, then 19 in 2017, 56
in 2018, and 75–92 from 2020. A per-radar passage-date trend needs about fifteen years, and exactly
one radar (`nldhl`, 2009–2025) has them.

**Robust to the thresholds, which is why it is stated this strongly.** "Usable" above means ≥200 of
a day's 288 five-minute profiles and ≥90 such days in the August–November window, and both bars were
swept: across profiles-per-day of 1/50/100/200 and days-per-autumn of 30/60/90/110, the count of
radars reaching fifteen autumns is 0 or 1 in every one of the sixteen combinations. At the
absolutely loosest reading — any data at all, any number of days — one radar reaches fifteen years
and seventy reach ten, and those seventy are radars with token coverage rather than usable seasons.

**What ENRAM can still do, which is not nothing:**

- **A cross-sectional comparison.** 2020–2025 gives **103 radars and 517 radar-autumns** with solid
  coverage. That supports comparing the *shape and timing* of European passage against the US over
  the overlap — a climatology comparison, not a trend comparison.
- **Test whether the method transfers.** The VPTS format carries `u`, `v`, `ff`, `dd` *and*
  `sd_vvp` per height bin, which is the velocity-variability term the full
  [Shi et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/condor/article/127/3/duaf020/8051150) mixture model
  needs and which the Dark Ecology daily product does not expose. The composition work could be
  done properly here rather than on a single reflectivity-weighted mean.
- **End the single-network monopoly** for anything descriptive.

**Cost, for when it is scheduled.** Monthly gzipped VPTS files run ~10 MB each; the full archive is
around 168 GB, and even an autumn-only, well-covered subset is tens of gigabytes. `eta` is in
cm² km⁻³, the same unit as Dark Ecology's reflectivity, so both networks can be integrated to
*identical* quantities rather than approximately comparable ones — which is the one genuinely good
piece of news here and the reason the integration is worth writing when the time comes.

**So it is deprioritised, not abandoned.** It was queued as the strongest single addition on the
strength of a claim that turned out to be false, and the honest consequence is that it drops behind
work that does not depend on it.

Two corrections to what this note originally claimed, both checked at the source on 2026-07-29:

- **The licence is CC BY 4.0, not CC0.** aloftdata.eu states "Content on this site is licensed
  under a CC BY 4.0 License". Redistribution is still permitted so the gate passes it, but with an
  attribution requirement that the registry entry has to carry. The gate now enforces
  redistribution independently of animal sensitivity, so a wrong licence here is a real failure
  mode rather than a footnote.
- **There is no pre-integrated nightly product.** The repository publishes vertical profile time
  series only — VPTS CSV and ODIM bird profiles. So ENRAM is *not* the drop-in second network this
  note implied: integrating over height and over night is work we have to write. The reference to
  match is Dark Ecology's, whose profiles are 100 m bins within 50 km of the station up to 3000 m,
  with `speed` and `direction` from a reflectivity-weighted VVP uniform-velocity fit.

A third thing follows from `adr/0006`: **the wind field for a European network cannot be NARR**,
which is North America only. That is the point at which a Copernicus CDS account becomes worth
asking for, and it should be decided deliberately rather than by a driver loader silently
defaulting to a North American reanalysis.

**Southern hemisphere and Africa.** The
[Southern African Bird Atlas Project 2](https://sabap2.birdmap.africa/) has run since 2007, open
access and mirrored on GBIF. Structurally it is repeated checklists per pentad, which is
`SURVEY_INDEX` with reporting rate as the index — the same shape FISHGLOB already exercises. This is
the one candidate that addresses southern hemisphere *and* tropics *and* a continent with no
representation at all.

**Three corrections, all checked against the GBIF and atlas APIs on 2026-07-30.** This note gave
the wrong dataset and the wrong size, and the access route it assumed does not work.

- **The key quoted above was SABAP1, not SABAP2.** `282d0ccb-4fa0-40f9-8593-105c77e88417` is the
  *Southern African Bird Atlas Project* — 5,053,399 records, 1953–1992 with the atlas core in
  1987–1991. SABAP2 is `906e6978-e292-4a8b-9c39-adf6bb0f3323`, with **25,687,526** records over
  2007–2026, not ~19 million. Both are CC BY 4.0. "African Bird Atlas Project"
  (`285550d0-…`) is a *metadata-only* dataset with zero records, and SABAP2 already carries Malawi,
  Zambia and Mozambique, so there is no separate 24-country occurrence dataset to ingest.
- **SABAP2's own archive is unreachable.** The endpoint GBIF advertises is an IPT 2.3.2 at
  `aduipt.uct.ac.za:8080`, and the host times out on the archive and on its small EML alike. SABAP1's
  archive, by contrast, is served by GBIF itself at `orphans.gbif.org` (133 MiB) because the
  publisher's IPT is gone — so the older atlas is the one that can be ingested today.
- **The atlas API works, and it is pooled.** `api.birdmap.africa/sabap2/v2/summary/species/{id}/
  project/sabap2` returns one row per pentad with its bounding box, `cards`, `records`,
  `reporting_rate`, twelve monthly rates and lifetime first-sighting statistics — 20,248 pentads
  under the full protocol and 22,251 ad-hoc, **separated**, which is the effort distinction that
  matters. But `reporting_rate` is computed over 2007–present as a whole and no per-year endpoint
  exists. So the API gives a static effort-controlled atlas, not a series.

**What that leaves.** Without a GBIF account, the change-detection design is **atlas against
atlas** — SABAP1 1987–1991 against SABAP2 pooled, which is the EBBA1→EBBA2 design and the standard
comparison in this literature — plus a genuinely new globe layer for southern Africa. A
*within*-SABAP2 trend needs the raw records, and the only working bulk route to those is the GBIF
occurrence download API, which requires a free account and mints a citable DOI per download. That is
a decision to take deliberately, like the Copernicus account above, rather than by assuming a route
that turns out to be dead.

**Marine, southern hemisphere.** No trawl series south of the equator is in FISHGLOB. South
African, Australian, New Zealand and Chilean demersal surveys exist and are the obvious next
place to look; none verified for access yet, so this stays a research item rather than a plan.

**Still absent entirely.** Asia, at every latitude and in every realm. South America, for anything
with a time axis. Terrestrial mammals anywhere — no `TRACK` or `DETECTION` source has been
ingested, so the terrestrial realm is currently empty of everything except what OBIS incidentally
carries.

## The rule for the models, which matters more than the source list

A model trained on North American radar and validated on held-out North American *years* will
report excellent skill and tell you nothing about whether it works anywhere else. Two commitments,
fixed here before any model exists:

1. **Held-out regions, not only held-out years.** Skill is reported under leave-one-network-out and
   leave-one-survey-out splits, on the held-out region specifically, never pooled with the training
   regions. A model that cannot be evaluated that way is not reported as general.
2. **A geographic novelty mask, by the same logic as the climate one.** The plan already requires
   every projection to ship a mask flagging extrapolation into unobserved climate space. Predicting
   into a region with no training data is the same error in a different coordinate, and gets the
   same treatment: shown as unsupported rather than shown as a number.

Phase 1b is already the empirical warning rather than a hypothetical one. Its 29 surveys **disagree
in the sign** of distribution shift — IE-IGFS at −0.22 °lat/decade against SWC-IBTS-4 at +0.26.
A model fitted on either would predict the wrong direction for the other. Regional transfer is not
a safe default here; it is the thing most likely to be wrong.

## Order of work

Nothing here is scheduled ahead of Phase 2a. When it is:

1. **SABAP1, then SABAP2** — southern hemisphere, tropics, a new continent, and `SURVEY_INDEX`
   again. SABAP1 first because it is the half that can be ingested without a new account.
2. Widen eBird beyond CONUS — a constant, not a source.
3. Research southern-hemisphere trawl series.
4. Only then a terrestrial source, which is a new realm and a new ethics surface.

ENRAM has left this list: one radar has fifteen usable autumns, so it cannot carry a trend at all,
and what it can still do is descriptive. It stays in the section above as a documented reassessment
rather than as queued work.
